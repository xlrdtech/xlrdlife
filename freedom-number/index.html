Personal + Business Financial Baseline

The Freedom
Number

Two numbers run your whole financial life. The magic number is what it costs to survive. The freedom number is what it costs to be free. Everything else — your pricing, your targets, your goals — is arithmetic once you know both.

01Split the cost in two

Most people fail here before they start. They add every expense into one pile, get a scary number, and learn nothing from it. Your money has two jobs, and they need separate books.

Bucket one

The cost to do you

What it costs to keep your life running if the business went quiet tomorrow.

  • Housing, utilities, phone
  • Groceries — food, not restaurants
  • Transport: payment, insurance, fuel, repair reserve
  • Health: premium, prescriptions, copay reserve
  • Minimum debt payments only
Bucket two

The cost to do business

What it costs to keep the machine running before you pay yourself a dime.

  • Software, hosting, domains, AI spend
  • Insurance, licenses, registration
  • Equipment replacement reserve
  • Marketing that is actually producing leads
  • Processing fees, bank fees, contractors

The rule that makes this work: every line gets marked essential or not. Essential means the lights go off without it. Restaurants are not essential. Groceries are. That single column is what separates your magic number from your freedom number — and almost nobody draws it.

03The formulas

Paste these straight into Google Sheets or Excel against the CSV columns below. Nothing here is proprietary — it is arithmetic most people were simply never shown.

MetricFormulaWhy it is built that way
Cost to do youSUMIFS(monthly,scope,"personal",essential,"yes")Essentials only — that is the whole point of the column
Cost to do businessSUMIFS(monthly,scope,"business",essential,"yes")Before owner pay, always

Revenue target= freedom / (1 - margin)Profit comes off the top, not the bottom

The gross-up is the one to understand. If you need $5,000 to live and tax is 28%, you do not need $6,400 — you need 5000 / 0.72 = $6,944. Reserving 28% of the need instead of grossing up leaves you 544 dollars short every single month, and you will not notice until the bill arrives.

LTV against CAC is where most operators fool themselves. Comparing lifetime gross value to acquisition cost makes almost any business look fundable. Run LTV at your real gross margin and the same business often reads under 3:1 — which is the truth you can act on before it costs you a quarter.

06The operating discipline

These are not numbers, they are the practices that keep the numbers trustworthy. Skip them and every figure above becomes a guess with a dollar sign in front of it.

ABCS

Accounting · Bookkeeping · Calculations · Coordination · Schedules · Spreadsheets

The template stack. One place where every figure is entered once and read everywhere, so no two documents disagree.

RevOps

Revenue operations

The wiring between marketing, sales and delivery — so a lead, a deal and an invoice are the same object at three stages, not three systems that never reconcile.

Financial investigation

Needs evaluation — assets and liabilities

A full look at monetary and economic position before any decision: what is owned, what is owed, what is actually producing, and what is quietly consuming.

Charter of accounts

The account taxonomy

Every dollar has exactly one correct home. Built once, before volume — retrofitting a chart of accounts across a year of transactions is a month you do not get back.

Reconciliation

Books against bank

Monthly, without exception. Unreconciled books are not records, they are estimates — and they fail the moment a lender or an auditor reads them.

"Reasonable salary"

Owner compensation

A defensible wage for the work actually performed, separate from owner's distribution. It is what makes payroll, proof of income and the tax position hold together.

07The account architecture

Money does not just need to be earned, it needs somewhere correct to land. Separate accounts are what turn intentions into arithmetic that cannot be spent by accident.

Sweep account

Deposits

Everything lands here first, then gets swept out to its purpose. One inbound door makes reconciliation and deposit history clean.

Tax account

10–20% offset and contributions

Swept aside on arrival, never at filing time. Pair it with the gross-up in section 03 so the reserve is actually sufficient rather than merely present.

Operations amount

Expenses · transfers · bills · costs · subscriptions

The machine's own account. If operations and personal share a balance, neither number above can be trusted.

Payroll accounts

"Reasonable salary" + owner's distribution

Two distinct flows, kept distinct. Wage is documented compensation; distribution is a return on ownership. Merging them costs you the paper trail.

Payroll provider

Documented proof of income

ADP, Paychex or equivalent — for verifiable income, employment verification and the data points capital consulting and compliance will ask for. Self-reported income is not the same instrument.

Tax certificate · business licensing

Primary NAICS with secondary codes, plus SIC

Industry classification is how institutions read you. Correct primary and secondary NAICS with SIC mapping drives market analysis, valuation comparables and stated income on applications — the foundation of a real banking relationship for financing and funding.

Reposits — living trust and trust EIN. Trust structures with their own EIN, and the 1099 series that reports against them (1099-A, -B, -C, -OID), are instruments that sit squarely in licensed territory. They belong in a plan built with a CPA and a tax attorney who put their own credentials behind it.
