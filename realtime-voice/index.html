Xen · voice architecture

Real-time full-duplex voice —
without rebuilding your rail

You already built the pieces: apple-stt in, omnimind (:4441) as the brain, ElevenLabs "East" out. The only thing missing is the real-time glue. You don't build that from scratch — and you don't have to pay a meter to run it.

01The answer: an open BYO orchestration layer

There's a whole category of open-source frameworks whose entire job is the "glue" — turn-taking, interruption, streaming — and they're bring-your-own-model. You keep every piece you built; they just conduct.

Recommended

LiveKit Agents

Apache-2.0, fully self-hostable. The open livekit-server runs on your own box with zero per-minute charge — you only pay if you use their Cloud. Nothing throttles you mid-task.

Its standout is the turn-detector: a small open-weight transformer that predicts end-of-turn from the meaning of your words, not silence. Runs CPU-local (<500MB), cut false interruptions ~39%.

# you keep all three pieces — the framework only conducts: apple-stt → custom stt.STT subclass omnimind → openai.LLM(base_url="http://localhost:4441/v1") East → elevenlabs.TTS(voice_id="East", model="eleven_flash_v2_5") # ~75ms
Equal on paper

Pipecat (Daily)

BSD-2, self-host free, frame-based pipeline that auto-cancels TTS on interruption. Turn-taking = Silero VAD + its own open smart-turn-v2 model. Just as capable; LiveKit edges it on WebRTC transport maturity and a more battle-tested semantic turn-detector.

Violates your constraints

Moshi / Kyutai

A genuine full-duplex speech-to-speech model (open weights) — but it replaces omnimind and speaks in its own voice, not East. It's the all-in-one path, not the keep-your-pieces path. Park it as a research watch.

02The real lesson: where the milliseconds go

Full-duplex "speed" is misunderstood. STT runs concurrently with your speech, so it adds ~0 — the clock only starts when you stop talking. From there:

Endpoint / turn-detectthe real gate — too tight cuts you off, too loose = dead air
~200 ms
LLM first token (TTFT)the dominant slice — everything waits on your first token
~150–300 ms
TTS first byte (ElevenLabs Flash)your East clone, kept fast
~75–100 ms
Transport / playback
~50 ms
End-of-speech → first audio
~600–800 ms

Production fleets report ~680ms p50 / ~1,180ms p95 for this cascaded shape.

The non-obvious win. The framework's value isn't a faster model — it's streaming + semantic endpointing. STT partials feed the LLM before you finish the sentence; TTS plays sentence one while sentence two is still generating; and the turn-detector reclaims the 500–800ms of dead air a naive silence timer wastes, because it knows "he's mid-thought, wait" vs "he's done, go." That perceived-latency reclaim is bigger than any single model swap.

03Why this fits you specifically

  • No meter, no flow-break. Self-hosted LiveKit (Apache-2.0) can't throttle you mid-task — the thing that destroys your flow doesn't exist here.
  • Nothing fragments. omnimind stays the brain, East stays your voice, apple-stt stays your ears. One glue layer added, zero pieces replaced.
  • The turn-detector runs on your box. Local, free weights, no cloud round-trip for the one decision that matters most — when to speak.

One thing to confirm before building: whether apple-stt emits streaming partials (vs one batch result at the end) — that determines how it wires in as a streaming STT plugin.
