Ten mailbox queries, ordered by how much they actually find. The insight that matters: failed-payment notices are a better inventory than receipts — a dead card keeps bouncing monthly and each bounce names the vendor and the exact amount, while a cancelled sub goes silent.

The Spark prompt

Paste this whole block into Spark's AI. It's written to make Spark do the sweep itself and hand back a table instead of a pile of threads.

Copy this →for Spark AI
Search my entire mailbox, all folders including archive and trash, going
back two years, and build me a complete inventory of every recurring
charge and subscription.

Look for all of these, not just receipts:
  - failed or declined payment notices ("was unsuccessful", "payment
    failed", "action required", "update your payment method")
  - renewal warnings ("will renew", "renews on", "auto-renew")
  - receipts and invoices
  - anything from Stripe, PayPal, Square, Paddle, Chargebee, FastSpring
  - Apple and Google Play subscription receipts
  - free trials that started and were never cancelled
  - cloud and hosting bills (Railway, Vercel, Netlify, Cloudflare,
    Hetzner, DigitalOcean, AWS, Supabase)
  - price-increase notices

Return one row per vendor with these columns:
  vendor | amount | billing cycle | last charge date | status | which
  email address it bills to

For status use exactly one of: ACTIVE, FAILING, CANCELLED, UNKNOWN.
Mark FAILING when a charge bounced and was retried and no later
success appears. Mark CANCELLED only if there is an explicit
cancellation email.

Then give me three separate lists at the bottom:
  1. Charges still being attempted against a card that is declining
  2. Subscriptions I have not opened an email from in over six months
  3. Anything where you could not determine the amount

Do not guess an amount. If it is not stated in the email, write
"not stated" and tell me which email to open to find it.
Sort the main table by annual cost, highest first.

Two things make this work: it asks for failed charges (most people only ask for receipts, which hides everything billed to a dead card), and it forbids guessing amounts — otherwise the AI fills the gaps with plausible fiction.

The query pack

Paste into the Gmail search bar as-is. Run them in this order — each one is written to catch what the one above it misses.

1 · Failed charges — the loudest signalhighest yield
subject:("was unsuccessful" OR "payment failed" OR "declined" OR "action required" OR "update your payment") newer_than:2y

Every one of these names a vendor and a dollar amount. Repeats mean the sub is still active and still trying to bill.

2 · Renewal warnings — catches the ones that never failhigh yield
subject:("will renew" OR "renews on" OR "auto-renew" OR "renewal notice" OR "subscription will") newer_than:1y

Legally required notices. These surface healthy subs that never bounce, so receipts alone would hide them.

3 · Receipts, the exact phrasingclean
subject:("your receipt from" OR "receipt for your payment" OR "payment receipt" OR "invoice from") newer_than:1y

Deliberately narrow. Broad receipt searches drown in newsletters — this one stays clean.

4 · Payment processors — the chokepointhigh yield
{from:stripe.com from:paypal.com from:squareup.com from:braintree from:chargebee from:paddle.com from:fastspring} newer_than:1y

Most SaaS bills through a handful of processors. Hit the processor and you catch vendors you forgot the name of.

5 · App-store subscriptions — invisible to card statements by nameoften missed
{from:apple.com from:google.com} subject:(receipt OR subscription OR "your invoice" OR purchase) newer_than:1y

Apple and Google bill as one lump. The itemization lives only in these emails.

6 · Free trials about to bitepreventive
subject:("trial" OR "trial ends" OR "trial expires" OR "welcome to") newer_than:6m

Catches the charge before it lands rather than after.

7 · Cancellations — so you don't chase ghostssubtractive
subject:("cancelled" OR "canceled" OR "has ended" OR "we're sorry to see you go" OR "subscription ended") newer_than:2y

Run this against the list from queries 1–5 and strike off anything already dead.

8 · Price hikes you agreed to by not replyingsneaky
subject:("price" OR "pricing update" OR "changes to your plan" OR "new rate") newer_than:1y

The amount you remember is often not the amount you're paying.

9 · Infrastructure metered billing — variable, so easy to missvariable cost
{from:railway.app from:vercel.com from:netlify.com from:cloudflare.com from:hetzner from:digitalocean from:aws-marketing from:supabase} newer_than:1y

Usage-based bills change every month, so they never look like a "subscription" in your head.

10 · The catch-all sweeplast pass
category:purchases OR label:^smartlabel_receipt newer_than:1y

Gmail's own auto-classifier. Run it last to catch whatever the nine above didn't phrase-match.
