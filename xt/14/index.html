WHAT ACTUALLY DEFINES IT (observed, not described secondhand)

1. LIGHT, NOT DARK. A pale cool grey-blue field. This is the opposite of the current

hitthe.link direction (near-black + neon green). Do not assume dark = premium.

2. HAIRLINE WIREFRAME. The subject — an igloo — is drawn in 1px white line only. No

fills, no gradients on the object, no material shading. Line IS the render.

3. ISOMETRIC / TECHNICAL-DRAWING GRAMMAR. True isometric projection, the language of an

architectural plate, not a hero render.

4. SCATTERED CONSTELLATION OF GEOMETRY. Dozens of faint wireframe planes and triangles

float around the subject at the same isometric angle — an exploded field, not a background.

5. TINY NUMERIC ANNOTATIONS. Small numbers label points throughout. It reads as a

measured drawing, which is what makes it feel engineered rather than decorated.

6. ONE FOCAL LIGHT. A single small bright bloom at the igloo's apex. Exactly one source of

emphasis in the entire frame.

7. ENORMOUS NEGATIVE SPACE + NEAR-ZERO CONTRAST. Most of the canvas is empty. The whole

image lives in a narrow value band. Restraint is the effect.

8. NO CHROME, NO PANELS, NO CARDS, NO SHADOW BOXES. Nothing sits on top of the scene.

(Directly relevant: the Gemini build's .glass-panel overlay is the anti-pattern.)

THE ONE-LINE READ

igloo.inc wins by subtraction and precision — a measured line drawing floating in space —

where the current hitthe.link wins by addition and intensity. Getting to his standard is

not "add more glow"; it is confidence enough to remove almost everything and let craft in the

linework carry it.

SCOPE

Applies to hitthe.link AND, in his words, all his landing pages. It is a consistency bar:

*"once we get that mastered consistently."* One page hitting it is not the goal.
