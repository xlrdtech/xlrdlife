Why this exists

CTO East has two visual grammars, and only one shipped. The distinction is not an interpretation — it is stated in the page's own source at cto-east/index.html:1215:

"the cutting of scenes like going back and forth between different angles, that was for real life, from a video of me for real. Like my likeness, NOT the avatar."
SubjectGrammarStatus
Avatar — procedural plasmaContinuous travel along a path, through portals. Time never stops.BUILT · live
Likeness — real videoFreeze-cut. Motion stops, then the angle changes. Different sides of him.NEVER STARTED

Measured on the live page: zero <video>, zero <img>, zero <canvas> in markup. There is no likeness surface at all. The render tool was the blocker on record — that blocker is gone.

Each keyed to a shot that already exists in the page, so renders drop into real slots. Freeze-cut throughout — hold, then hard cut. Never an orbit.

Insertion mechanics

  • Video texture, parented to the world. Each render becomes a THREE.VideoTexture on a plane parented to TRAVEL, never to the avatar — the same lesson already recorded at line 1669.
  • The freeze primitive already exists. TF.v == 0 at line 1057 stops motion mid-arc for the cut. The likeness plane reuses it rather than inventing a second timing system.
  • The camera does not move during a cut. The plane swaps its map; the camera holds. That single rule is the whole difference from the avatar's grammar.
Identity guard — carried forward unchanged
A likeness render is his own face and his own name. It never reaches a public URL without his explicit go-ahead on that specific asset, and never as anything implying a statement he did not make. This page therefore publishes prompts and empty slots only.
