L7S GAP ANALYSIS — live l7sinc.com vs. what converts

Prepared 2026-08-01 by Lynn, for East + Luckie. Evidence: full scrape of the live l7sinc.com

(682 lines) taken today, plus the four rebuilt draft pages. Nothing here is guessed — every

finding is quoted from the live site.

THE HEADLINE: the intake gates every visitor on buying a credit-monitoring product

The live intake asks, as a required field:

"Do you have active credit monitoring?" *(required)* — plus a required provider dropdown,
and the line "Credit monitoring is required for assessment."

Consequence: no prospect can reach an R&R™ session without first signing up for a

third-party monitoring service. That is a paywall in front of your own free consultation, at the

exact moment a lead is warmest. This is the most likely reason the site does not convert.

It also contradicts the same site's own FAQ:

*"Do you directly provide credit repair? No."*

Recommendation: make monitoring OPTIONAL (an "if you already have it, name it" field), and

say plainly that it is not required to book. The rebuilt drafts already say

*"active credit monitoring is NOT required"* — per Luckie's own correction.

LEGAL CONFLICT — worth fixing before more traffic, not after

The live Terms of Service says L7S provides:

*"business consulting, financial guidance, credit restoration, educational resources"*

…while the same site states L7S is NOT a credit repair company.

"Credit restoration" is language regulators read as credit repair, which pulls in CROA (and

FTC) obligations: required written contracts, mandatory disclosures, a 3-day cancellation right,

and no advance fees. Saying both things at once is the worst of both worlds.

Recommendation: pick one position and make every page agree. If L7S is education + systems

(which the offer looks like), strike "credit restoration" from the Terms. Have counsel confirm

before the next campaign — this is not a copy nit.

Pick one name per thing, everywhere. "Loan Hero" also matches the GHL sub-account already named

for lead routing, which argues for standardizing on Loan Hero.

PRICING — the live site and the spec are two different businesses

  • Live: $39/mo membership + $97 / $197 / $397 / $1,497 digital upgrades.
  • Spec (Luckie's, now in the drafts): Low $7–$333 · Mid $777–$7,777 · High $11,777–$33,777 ·

    • Enterprise $27,777–$100,000+.

    That is a 20–70× gap. The live page sells a product ladder; the spec sells a consulting/JV

    firm. Both can exist — but not on one page with no bridge, because a visitor cannot tell which

    company they are buying from. Decide which is the front door and make the other a clearly

    separate track.

    FRAMEWORKS — named but never defined

    Live site names these with no expansion anywhere: **P.A.C.T.S.™ · B.E.S.T. M.A.P.S.™ ·

    T.H.I.S. I.S. V.I.P.™ · W.A.T.E.R.™ · E.A.T.S.™**. Trademark-style acronyms with no definition

    read as noise to a cold visitor. Either define them on-page or cut them from public copy.

    (The drafts list them by name only, explicitly not guessed.)
